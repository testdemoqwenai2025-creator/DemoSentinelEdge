Four structural problems

The opportunity is not another sensor. It is the universal substrate.

Every industrial IoT deployment we have studied in the last decade hits the same four walls. They are not bugs in any one platform — they are the consequence of trying to bolt intelligence onto plumbing that was never designed for it. The four problems below compound: each one makes the next harder to solve.

01 · Protocol Heterogeneity

A single factory floor commonly runs Modbus, OPC-UA, Profinet, EtherCAT, MQTT, CoAP, and proprietary serial — all at once. Bridging them with semantic understanding, not just byte translation, is still a research problem.

Today this work is done by 30-person system-integrator teams over 6 months per site. The cost: $400k-$1.2M per factory onboarding, before any AI is deployed. Worse: the mapping tables are brittle — any change in the underlying PLC tags breaks downstream analytics.

02 · Calibration Drift

Every sensor degrades. A 10,000-node deployment costs an average of $1.4M per year in truck rolls to recalibrate. No platform has solved self-calibrating networks at scale — redundant physics + ML + sparse ground truth is the open frontier.

The problem is worse than it sounds: undetected drift produces confident wrong readings, which propagate through the analytics layer and erode operator trust in the entire system. Most plants deal with this by manually flagging any reading that "looks wrong" — which is exactly the kind of human-in-the-loop friction AI was supposed to eliminate.

Status quo cost

$1.4M/year for a 10k-node mesh · undetected drift erodes operator trust in the entire system

03 · Edge-Cloud Workload Placement

Most platforms hardcode "ML runs in cloud, rules run at edge." No one dynamically reassigns workloads based on bandwidth cost, latency need, and model accuracy drift in real time. Result: 38% of industrial AI pilots stall at the PoC-to-production gap.

The deeper issue: the optimal placement changes minute to minute. Bandwidth cost spikes during peak hours. Battery state fluctuates. Model accuracy drifts as distribution shifts. A hardcoded placement that's optimal at 9am is wrong by 11am. Without a scheduler, you either over-pay (everything in cloud) or under-serve (everything on edge).

Status quo cost

38% PoC-to-production stall rate · 30-50% of inference efficiency left on the table

04 · Unlabeled Industrial Data

92% of industrial IoT data is unlabeled. Existing ML requires months of labeled failure examples per asset class. The frontier is zero-shot / few-shot anomaly detection across sensor types the model has never seen.

The root cause: failures are rare by design. A well-maintained compressor might fault once every 5 years. By the time you have enough labeled examples to train a bespoke model, the asset has been replaced. This is why most industrial ML projects stall at pilot — they can't accumulate training data fast enough.

Status quo cost

92% of industrial IoT data unlabeled · bespoke models need 6-18 months of failure examples to train

Why we don't go horizontal first

The $547B TAM is seductive but unfocused. Every horizontal IoT platform that's tried to attack it (GE Predix, Google Cloud IoT, Cisco Jasper) has either collapsed or stalled. Vertical wedges concentrate resources, build reference customers, and compound the foundation model — that's how Cognite (industrial), Augury (vibration), and Planet Labs (EO) each got to $1B+ valuations.

Why now

Three structural shifts converged in 2024-2025.

The four problems above are not new. They've been known for a decade. What changed in the last 18 months is that the tools to solve them finally became real. We are 18 months into this window; it will close within 36 months as incumbents react.

1. Time-series foundation models became real

Chronos (Amazon, 2024), TimeGPT (Nixtla, 2024), Moment (CMU, 2024), Lag-Llama — pre-trained general time-series models can now match or beat bespoke models. The field went from "every customer trains their own" to "pre-trained, few-shot adapted" in less than 18 months. Industrial pre-training corpora (NASA bearings, Case Western Reserve, SECOM) are public for the first time.

2. Edge AI silicon dropped below $50 at scale

Hailo-8 ($45, 26 TOPS, 2.5W), Google Coral Edge TPU ($35, 4 TOPS, 2W), Qualcomm QCS6490 — running real ML on every sensor is now economically viable. Two years ago the cheapest viable edge AI chip was $200+. This unlocks per-sensor intelligence, not just per-gateway.

3. LLMs can ground on multi-modal sensor data

A natural-language query over a sensor fabric was impossible in 2022. Today, retrieval-augmented generation over graph-structured sensor data is a proven pattern. We extend it with causal inference (PC algorithm + do-calculus) — turning "vibration correlates with failure" into "vibration caused failure."

The 36-month window

AWS IoT, Azure IoT, and Cognite are all reacting. They have ~36 months before our vertical wedge compounds become uncatchable. After that, the foundation model's data network effect (every new customer improves the model for all customers) becomes the moat.

▼ The Compound Effect

Why these four problems compound.

Each problem, solved in isolation, delivers value. But solved together — by the same platform — the value compounds. Each solution feeds the others. This is why AISensorEdgeComp is a platform, not a point solution.

Protocol Heterogeneity → enables everything else

The canonical schema (Problem 1) is the foundation. Without clean, normalized data, the self-calibration mesh (Problem 2) has nothing to calibrate, the liquid placement scheduler (Problem 3) has nothing to route, and the TS-FM (Problem 4) has nothing to infer on. Protocol normalization happens first, at the edge, before any other processing.

→ Feeds: calibration mesh, placement scheduler, TS-FM

Calibration Drift → enables trust in AI

The self-calibration mesh (Problem 2) produces calibration_confidence (0.0-1.0) on every measurement. This score feeds into the TS-FM (Problem 4) — low-confidence readings are down-weighted in the inference window. It also feeds the placement scheduler (Problem 3) — low-confidence readings from a drifting sensor may be routed to the more accurate cloud model for a second opinion.

→ Feeds: placement scheduler (confidence weighting), TS-FM (input quality)

Edge-Cloud Placement → enables scale + ESG

The liquid placement scheduler (Problem 3) routes inference jobs dynamically. It uses the TS-FM's confidence (Problem 4) as an input — when edge model accuracy drifts, it routes to the cloud model. It also uses the calibration_confidence (Problem 2) — low-confidence readings may trigger a cloud fallback. The scheduler's carbon-aware routing is our ESG differentiator.

← Uses: TS-FM confidence, calibration confidence → → Enables: 72h edge autonomy, carbon-aware routing

Unlabeled Data (TS-FM) → enables zero-shot from day 1

The TS-FM (Problem 4) runs zero-shot anomaly detection on the canonical data (Problem 1), weighted by calibration confidence (Problem 2), routed by the liquid scheduler (Problem 3). Its confidence scores feed back into the scheduler — high-confidence edge inferences stay local; low-confidence ones escalate to cloud. The TS-FM also learns from federated gradients across all customers, compounding accuracy over time.

← Uses: canonical data, calibration confidence, placement routing → → Feeds: placement decisions (confidence), federated model improvement
