Ask the Founders

The Q&A that happens after the pitch. These are the 20 questions investors ask most often — answered honestly by the founding team. No marketing, just engineering and business reality.

Q01How do you compete with AWS IoT and Azure IoT? They have unlimited resources.
+

AWS and Azure sell plumbing — ingest, storage, rules engines. They don't sell intelligence. No TS-FM, no Graph RAG, no causal inference, no federated learning, no carbon-aware routing, no self-calibration mesh. Their customers build those themselves (or don't, which is why 38% of AI pilots stall).

We integrate with them — customers can use AWS S3 or IoT Core as ingest. But the reasoning layer is ours. The moat isn't features; it's the federated network effect. Every customer's data improves the TS-FM for all customers. AWS can't copy this because their customers don't trust them with data gradients — and rightly so, given AWS's business model is lock-in.

Dr. Aarav Kapoor, CEO
Q02Why can't Cognite or Augury just add a foundation model and catch up?
+

They can add a model — but they can't add the federated learning loop. Cognite is a data ops platform; their customers use it because data stays in their tenant. Augury is a vibration SaaS; their customers use it because it's turnkey. Neither can ask their customers to share gradients across the network — the trust model is different.

Also, our TS-FM isn't just a model — it's three architectural choices (industrial-only pretraining, sensor fusion cross-attention, causal-aware loss) that took Dr. Jian Liu's team 18 months to get right. Adding a generic TS model is easy; getting 0.89 AUC-ROC zero-shot on industrial data is not. See our TS-FM architecture blog post for the ablations.

Q05Your TS-FM beats Chronos by 0.07 AUC-ROC. Is that statistically significant?
+

Yes — on our held-out test set (1,000 labeled industrial events, 6 modalities, 7 asset classes), the 0.89 vs 0.82 delta has p < 0.001 via bootstrap (10,000 resamples). But the more important point: the 0.07 AUC-ROC delta compounds at the decision threshold. At 0.85 confidence threshold, 0.89 AUC gives you 91% precision at 85% recall. 0.82 AUC gives you 78% precision at 85% recall. That's the difference between "operators trust the alerts" and "operators ignore the alerts."

The ablations show where the 0.07 comes from: causal-aware loss (+0.04), industrial-only training (+0.02), sensor fusion (+0.03). Remove any one and you're back at Chronos level. See the blog post for the full ablation table.

Dr. Jian Liu, Chief Scientist
Q06How do you handle model drift in production? What if the TS-FM degrades over time?
+

Three monitors run continuously: feature drift (Population Stability Index per sensor, alarm at PSI > 0.2), confidence distribution (output confidence over time, alert on significant shifts), and ground-truth feedback (when actual failures occur, compare to predictions, update per-asset-class confusion matrix). When PSI exceeds 0.2, we trigger a federated retraining round — each customer contributes gradients (with DP noise ε=1.0), aggregated in SGX, global model updated. Customers get the improved model within 24 hours.

This is why the federated learning loop matters — it's not just a moat; it's the mechanism that keeps the model accurate as the world changes. Without it, you'd retrain quarterly (if at all) and drift silently between retrainings.

Dr. Jian Liu, Chief Scientist
Q07The causal inference claim is interesting but bold. How do you validate it in practice?
+

Validation is the hardest part — you can't deliberately break bearings to test if your intervention prediction was right. We use historical interventions: when operators actually did intervene (e.g., reduced compressor load), did the outcome match the prediction?

We have 100+ historical interventions from design-partner deployments. The agreement rate between predicted and actual outcomes is 0.83 — meaning 83% of the time, when the system said "reducing load will extend bearing life by 400 hours," the operator reduced load and the bearing lasted 380-420 hours. The other 17% is where the causal graph was incomplete (a hidden confounder we hadn't modeled). Each miss improves the PC algorithm's skeleton. See our causal inference blog post.

JL
Dr. Jian Liu, Chief Scientist
Q08Why edge compute? Cloud is cheaper per FLOP. Isn't edge a step backward?
+

Cloud is cheaper per FLOP but more expensive per useful inference when you factor in bandwidth, latency, and autonomy. For 10,000 sensors streaming at 1 Hz, that's 2 GB/hour to the cloud at $0.09/GB = $180/day in bandwidth alone. Edge inference at 2.5W (Hailo-8) costs $0.05/day in electricity. The edge is 3,600× cheaper for the inference part.

More importantly: safety-critical inferences can't depend on WAN. Gas leak detection on an offshore platform needs <100ms. Cloud round-trip is 200-500ms. If the satellite link is down (which happens), the platform is blind. With edge autonomy (72 hours), the platform keeps running. Cloud is a co-processor, not a dependency. See the edge-cloud placement deep dive.

Q10What's your gross margin? How does it scale?
+

Software gross margin: 78-85% at scale (main cost is cloud compute for inference + storage, which is ~15-20% of revenue at 10k+ sensors). Edge hardware is pass-through (we don't manufacture — customers buy Hailo-8/Jetson from distributors). At 10,000 sensors: ~$25K/month revenue, ~$4K/month cloud cost → 84% gross margin. At 100,000 sensors: ~$250K/month revenue, ~$25K/month cloud cost → 90% gross margin (compute scales sub-linearly due to edge processing). We expect to reach 85% blended gross margin by end of Year 2.

Q19What if the federated learning moat doesn't compound as expected? What if customers opt out?
+

Even if 50% of customers opt out of federated learning, the model still improves from the other 50%. And the opt-out customer still gets a good model (0.89 AUC zero-shot) — they just don't contribute to the compounding. The risk isn't "what if everyone opts out" (they won't — the discount incentive is real) — it's "what if the model doesn't improve fast enough from federated gradients to create a structural gap."

In our simulations, after 36 months of federated learning across 20 customers (each contributing ~5M hours of data), the model's AUC on unseen asset classes improves from 0.89 to ~0.94. That's a 0.05 delta — meaningful but not insurmountable for a well-funded competitor. The real moat isn't the accuracy delta; it's the data network effect — after 36 months, we have 20 customers' worth of industrial patterns, and a new competitor starts from zero.

Q20If you could only tell investors one thing, what would it be?

The four problems compound. Every IoT platform solves one problem — maybe two. We solve four, and each solution feeds the others. The canonical schema enables the self-calibration mesh. The calibration confidence feeds the placement scheduler. The placement scheduler routes the TS-FM. The TS-FM's confidence feeds back into the scheduler. And the federated learning loop improves the TS-FM across all customers.

This is why we're a platform, not a point solution. A point solution company can solve one problem well. A platform company solves four problems that compound — and the compounding is the moat. After 36 months of customer data compounding, the accuracy + trust + cost-efficiency gap vs any point solution is structural. See the compound effect →
