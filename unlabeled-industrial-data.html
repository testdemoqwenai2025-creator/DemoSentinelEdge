Unlabeled Industrial Data

92% of industrial IoT data is unlabeled. Failures are rare by design. Bespoke ML models need 6-18 months of labeled failure examples per asset class. The TS-FM solves this with zero-shot anomaly detection — 0.89 AUC-ROC with zero labeled data.

The Pain

You can't label failures that haven't happened yet.

92% of industrial IoT data is unlabeled. Normal operation data is abundant — billions of readings of "everything is fine." Failure data is vanishingly rare — a well-maintained compressor faults once every 5 years. By the time you accumulate enough labeled failures to train a bespoke model, the asset has been replaced.

This is the structural reason most industrial ML projects stall at pilot. They can't accumulate training data fast enough. Each new asset class starts from zero labels. Each new factory starts from zero labels. The labeling problem compounds across every dimension of scale.

Root Cause

Failures are rare by design.

The root cause is not technological — it's structural. Industrial systems are designed to NOT fail. A well-maintained compressor might fault once in 5 years. A pipeline might leak once in 30 years. A transformer might fail once in 20 years. This is good engineering — but it's a disaster for supervised ML, which requires hundreds of labeled examples of each failure mode to train a model.

Why Existing Solutions Fail

Four approaches — all blocked by the label problem.

1. Supervised ML

Needs labeled data. Can't start for months. Each new asset class = zero labels = restart from scratch. 92% of industrial data is unlabeled.

2. Semi-Supervised

Still needs some labels. Pseudo-labeling introduces noise. Doesn't solve the fundamental rarity of failure events.

3. Transfer Learning

Doesn't transfer across industries. Vibration patterns in oil & gas ≠ mining ≠ chemical. Cross-domain transfer degrades accuracy below usable thresholds.

4. Rules-Based

Threshold-based (vibration >8 mm/s → alert). High false positive rate. Doesn't generalize. Every new asset class needs new rules — manual, expert-dependent.

Our Approach

The TS-FM: zero-shot anomaly detection.

The TS-FM (Time-Series Foundation Model) is a 350M-parameter causal transformer pretrained on 50M hours of public industrial time-series. It learns general time-series patterns (trends, seasonality, regime shifts, distribution changes) that transfer to ANY new asset class — without any labeled failures.

At inference time, the model flags distributional anomalies — readings that deviate from the learned normal pattern. No labeled failures needed. Zero-shot. From day one of deployment.

Three architectural deltas vs. Chronos-B1

1. Industrial-only pretraining

Trained exclusively on industrial data (NASA bearings, CWRU, SECOM, ARPA-E, UCR/UEA). No general web time-series (stocks, weather) that dilute the industrial signal. +0.04 AUC-ROC vs. mixed training.

2. Sensor fusion cross-attention

Cross-attention layers every 6th transformer block attend across modalities within the same time window. Vibration + temperature + pressure fusion catches faults that single-modality models miss. +0.03 AUC-ROC.

3. Causal-aware loss

PC algorithm skeleton constrains the loss function. Penalizes predictions violating conditional independence. The model learns physics, not just correlations. +0.04 AUC-ROC — biggest single contributor.

🏥Hospital ICU — Rare Sepsis Events

Sepsis occurs in ~0.2% of ICU patients. Traditional ML: needs 500+ labeled events = 250,000 patients.
