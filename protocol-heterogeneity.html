Protocol Heterogeneity

A single factory floor runs Modbus, OPC-UA, Profinet, EtherCAT, MQTT, CoAP, and proprietary serial — all at once. Bridging them with semantic understanding has historically required 30-person SI teams and 6 months per site, at $400K-$1.2M. We automated it.

The Pain

16 protocols. Zero semantic agreement.

A typical industrial facility runs 6-12 different communication protocols simultaneously, each born from a different era, vendor ecosystem, and design philosophy. Modbus (1979) gives you register 40001 — but doesn't tell you if that's temperature, pressure, or motor speed. OPC-UA (2008) gives you rich metadata — but encoded in its own information model. MQTT (1999) gives you a topic string — but the semantic meaning is implicit in naming conventions that vary by vendor.

The cost of resolving this heterogeneity — turning raw bytes from any protocol into meaning that an AI can reason over — is the single largest barrier to industrial AI deployment. It is why most industrial AI projects stall at pilot. They can't afford to onboard the second factory.

Root Cause

The problem isn't bytes — it's meaning.

The problem isn't just "different protocols on the same floor." It's that each protocol represents data differently at the semantic level:

  • Modbus gives you register 40001 containing a 16-bit integer. Is that temperature in °C? Pressure in psi? Motor speed in RPM? The protocol doesn't tell you. You need an external mapping document — usually an Excel spreadsheet maintained by hand.
  • OPC-UA gives you ns=2;s=Line3.CompressorA.OutletPressure with a description, data type, engineering unit, and range. Rich — but encoded in the OPC-UA information model, not a universal format.
  • MQTT gives you a topic string and JSON payload. The semantic meaning is implicit in the topic structure — but there's no standard for how to structure it.
  • LoRaWAN gives you raw bytes. Decoding requires a device-specific decoder function. Two different soil probe manufacturers produce completely different payloads for the same physical quantity.
Why Existing Solutions Fail

Three approaches — all insufficient.

1. Manual Tag Mapping

30-person SI team creates an Excel spreadsheet mapping every tag. Brittle: breaks on PLC tag changes. Expensive: $800K per site. Unscalable: 2nd factory takes as long as the 1st.

2. Protocol-Specific Gateways

Each protocol gets its own gateway from a different vendor. No semantic unification: the aggregation layer merges bytes, not meanings. Vendor lock-in: 12 gateways × 4 vendors = 144 failure modes.

3. Cloud-Only Normalization

AWS IoT / Azure IoT: send raw data to cloud, normalize there. Bandwidth cost: $180/day for 10k sensors. Latency: 200-500ms RTT. No edge autonomy: blind on WAN loss.

Our Approach

From byte-level to semantic-level.

The fundamental shift is moving the normalization from byte-level translation (Protocol A bytes → Protocol B bytes) to semantic-level understanding (any protocol → canonical meaning). The canonical meaning is a single JSON/Avro/Protobuf record that every downstream consumer operates on, regardless of which protocol produced the data.

Three pillars

1. Unified Semantic Data Model

A canonical schema that every measurement normalizes to — regardless of source protocol. Published as Avro + Protobuf, Apache 2.0, versioned with Schema Registry. Reduces integration from O(protocols × consumers) to O(protocols × 1).

2. AI-Driven Automation

LLM-assisted ontology mapping replaces 30-person SI teams. Fine-tuned on OPC-UA info models, Modbus conventions, 200+ customer mappings. Human-in-the-loop: LLM proposes, engineers approve. Full audit trail for SOC 2.

3. Flexible Middleware

Protocol-agnostic edge gateways that ingest from any of 16 protocols and emit canonical records. Stateless, horizontally scalable, 72h edge autonomy. Runs on Hailo-8, Jetson, Coral, RPi, or $1 MCUs.
