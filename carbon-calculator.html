How the carbon calculator works

The math, transparently.

Cloud inference energy

power_gpu_w × time_per_inference_s × inferences_per_year × PUE / 3.6M

GPU power (W) × time per inference (s) × annual inferences × PUE factor (1.12) → kWh. Multiplied by grid carbon intensity (gCO2/kWh) → kg CO2/year.

Edge inference energy

power_edge_w × time_per_inference_s × inferences_per_year / 3.6M

Edge silicon power (2.5W for Hailo-8 vs 150W for A10G) × time per inference → kWh. No PUE (edge devices don't have datacenter overhead). 60× lower power.

Liquid placement factor

edge_share × edge_carbon + cloud_share × cloud_carbon

Our scheduler routes 70% to edge, 30% to cloud (average). When grid carbon is high (peak hours), more goes to edge. When renewable % is high (midday solar), more goes to cloud. Carbon-aware routing.

Equivalents

1 kg CO2 = ~4 km driven by an average car. Annual savings expressed as car-km and trees needed to sequester the equivalent carbon (1 tree absorbs ~22 kg CO2/year).

Why this matters for ESG investors

Carbon-aware inference is a differentiator.

ESG-conscious investors (CalPERS, Generation IM, Breakthrough Energy) ask about carbon footprint. Most IoT platforms have no answer. We do:

  • Liquid placement already factors carbon intensity — the scheduler has a 5% weight on grid carbon when routing inference jobs
  • Edge inference is 60× lower power — Hailo-8 at 2.5W vs A10G at 150W
  • No datacenter PUE — edge devices don't have cooling/lighting overhead
  • Renewable-aware routing — when grid renewable % is high, more inference goes to cloud; when carbon intensity spikes, more goes to edge

AWS IoT and Azure IoT do not offer carbon-aware inference routing. This is a structural ESG advantage that compounds as carbon regulations tighten.
