Differential privacy — what's happening
Each customer computes a gradient on their local data (raw sensor readings never leave their boundary). Gaussian noise is added to the gradient (ε=1.0 per round). The noised gradient is sent to the secure aggregator, which sums all gradients inside an Intel SGX enclave. The platform operator cannot see individual gradients — only the aggregate sum. The global model is updated and broadcast back to all customers. After 100 rounds, the cumulative privacy budget is exhausted and training pauses (or ε resets with consent).

Why this is the moat

AWS IoT can't copy this.

Why AWS IoT / Azure IoT can't compete

Horizontal cloud platforms sell infrastructure. They cannot ask their customers to share data — even as gradients — because customers don't trust them. AWS makes money from customer lock-in; we make money from customer data compounding. These are opposite incentives.

Why vertical specialists can't compete

Augury, Cognite, Satelligence — each goes deep in one vertical. They have great models for one domain. But our federated model learns across all customers, in all verticals. After 36 months of compounding, our cross-vertical accuracy will be unreachable by any single-vertical player.

Why the customer says yes

Customer contributes gradients only — never raw data. Differential privacy (ε=1.0) means the gradient reveals almost nothing about any individual sensor reading. SGX secure aggregation means we (the platform operator) can't see the gradient either. The customer gets a better model without giving up data.

The 36-month window

Every round, every customer improves the model for every other customer. This compounds. After 36 months of customers contributing gradients, the accuracy gap vs. a non-federated competitor is structural — they would need 36 months of customer data to catch up, and they don't have our customers.
